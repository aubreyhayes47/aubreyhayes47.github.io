What a focused audit actually looks like.

This is a representative example of the kind of plain-English diagnosis a small business receives. It is intentionally specific enough to act on, without pretending to be a review of a real client.

Important: This page is an illustrative sample, not a client case study and not a claim of measured results.

Example: the first five opportunities

A real audit would use the business's public website, offer, location, and preferred lead path.

01 / CLARITY

Make the first screen answer “what do you do?”

A generic headline makes a visitor spend effort figuring out whether the business serves their problem and location.

Possible rewrite

“Same-day HVAC repair in Memphis — call for a clear appointment window.”

02 / ACTION

Give the page one obvious next step

Several competing buttons can delay a visitor who is ready to call, book, or request an estimate.

Test first

Use one primary “Call now” or “Request an appointment” action above the fold and repeat it after the service explanation.

03 / TRUST

Put proof beside the decision

Reviews, service-area details, licenses, warranties, and response expectations work best near the point where the visitor decides to contact the business.

Move closer

Add two specific review excerpts and the service area directly below the primary call-to-action.

04 / MOBILE

Protect the thumb-sized path

On a phone, a visitor should not need to zoom, hunt through menus, or scroll past a banner to call.

Check

Keep the primary action visible, readable, and reachable without opening a menu or filling a long form.

05 / FRICTION

Ask only for information needed to respond

Long forms can lose a lead before the business has a chance to start the conversation.

Reduce

Start with name, contact method, service needed, and preferred time; collect details later.

The final page is prioritized, not overwhelming.

The client leaves with a short sequence rather than a 40-page redesign proposal.

  1. Today: clarify the first-screen headline and primary action.
  2. Next: place one trust signal beside the action and remove competing choices.
  3. Then: test the complete path on a phone from landing page to contact.
